About This Program

Next Level is a structured, six-session leadership development experience designed for clinicians who have transitioned into non-clinical or management roles and are ready to operate at the next tier of organizational influence. Where Level 1 focused on making the transition, Level 2 focuses on performing at the top of it.

Every session follows the same pattern: a practical framework is introduced, applied live in a structured scenario, refined through peer and facilitator feedback, and closed with a tangible output you keep and use.

"Your clinical skills have direct application to broad areas of business. This program provides you with the vocabulary, practical experience, and confidence to translate those skills into strategic business outcomes that are highly sought after."

About This Map

The DPS Leadership Proficiency Map is a structured self-assessment designed for professionals building executive influence. It is designed less as a performance evaluation and more as a mirror.

It is completed twice: once at program kickoff to establish a clear baseline, and again at program close to capture measurable growth. The gap between those two data points is the most concrete evidence of your development and the foundation for your Executive Positioning Summary.

Equally important to the scores themselves is the Tendency Marker. For each domain, you will indicate whether that area feels like a natural strength, a growth edge, or neutral ground.

1
Read the domain definition and the four behavioral indicators. Sit with them before rating yourself.
2
Read each proficiency level description carefully. Select the level that most accurately reflects your current, consistent behavior — not your aspirational behavior or your best day.
3
Mark your Tendency: Natural Tendency (this comes easily to me), Growth Edge (this requires deliberate effort), or Neutral (somewhere in between).
4
Record your Pre-Program Score. At program close, re-assess and record your Post-Program Score. The delta is your evidence of growth.

Strategic Clarity
Pillar 1: Strategic Thinking & Decision-Making

Post: —
Definition

The ability to identify what actually matters, frame decisions with tradeoffs visible, and recommend a clear course of action even when information is incomplete or the stakes are high.

Behavioral Indicators

  • Frames problems with a clear priority hierarchy before proposing solutions
  • Identifies the real decision at stake, not just the surface-level presenting question
  • Makes recommendations that acknowledge tradeoffs rather than presenting one-sided advocacy
  • Acts with directional confidence under uncertainty without waiting for perfect information

Emerging
I tend to respond to situations as they are presented to me. I have difficulty stepping back to identify the actual decision being made or the priorities at play. I often need others to frame the problem before I can contribute a substantive recommendation.
2
Developing
I am beginning to ask what matters most before responding, but my prioritization is inconsistent. I sometimes confuse urgent with important, and my recommendations do not always make the tradeoffs visible.
3
Proficient
I consistently frame problems with priorities visible before proposing action. My recommendations include explicit tradeoffs, and I am comfortable acting on partial information when the direction is clear enough.
4
Distinguished
Strategic clarity is a visible and distinguishing characteristic of how I work. I am frequently sought out to help frame complex or ambiguous situations and can build that capability in others in real time.

Definition

The ability to engage constructively when disagreement, friction, or resistance arises — holding a clear position with confidence while keeping relationships intact and organizational goals in view.

Behavioral Indicators

  • Maintains a clear position under pressure while remaining genuinely open to new information

The ability to select meaningful metrics, interpret them accurately, and build credible advocacy narratives that tie individual or team work to organizational outcomes in the language of business impact.

  • Reads and interprets operational and financial data with sufficient fluency to inform recommendations

Leadership Poise Profiles

You are not defined by your profile; rather, your proficiency scores reveal a development plan. The six profiles below are drawn from recurring patterns in how executive leaders carry their strengths. A profile is not a ceiling — it is a starting point with a name.

The Strategic Architect
D1 + D5 highest
Signature Strength

You are unusually good at seeing through complexity to identify what actually matters and building the evidence to support it. Your data-grounded advocacy is difficult to ignore.

Watch For

Relational influence and conflict navigation may not come as naturally. Technically sound arguments can lose traction in emotionally charged environments. Invest in D3 and D4.

The Organizational Diplomat
D3 + D4 highest
Signature Strength

You navigate people and complexity with an intuition most professionals never fully develop. You know how to sequence influence and bring competing stakeholders toward alignment.

Watch For

Data framing and strategic clarity may be growth edges. Without metrics to back your influence, it can be perceived as relationship-dependent rather than evidence-based. Invest in D1 and D5.

The Executive Communicator
D2 + D7 highest
Signature Strength

You command rooms. Your presence is deliberate, your delivery is clear, and your composure under pressure earns trust that others have to work harder to establish.

Watch For

Influence strategy and developing others may be areas where your natural presence is not enough on its own. Ensure communication is backed by intentional stakeholder planning. Invest in D3 and D6.

The Team Catalyst
D6 + D3 highest
Signature Strength

You bring out the best in the people around you and build coalitions that others can't. You create psychological safety and understand that organizational outcomes are built through people.

Watch For

Data fluency and strategic framing may be the edges that limit your executive credibility. The strength you build in others may not be visible to leadership because it isn't yet quantified. Invest in D1 and D5.

The Resilient Leader
D4 + D7 highest
Signature Strength

You handle pressure, setback, and conflict with a composure that others notice and rely on. When situations escalate or become ambiguous, people look to you. That stability is a rare and genuinely valuable leadership asset.

Watch For

Influence building and data framing may be less developed. Composure earns respect, but translating that respect into organizational movement requires intentional influence strategy. Invest in D3 and D5.

The Balanced Operator
Scores within 1 point across most domains
Signature Strength

You are well-rounded. You bring consistent, reliable performance across all seven domains without an obvious vulnerability. This signals deep self-awareness and adaptability.

Watch For

Well-rounded professionals sometimes lack a signature domain. As you grow, consider deliberately developing one domain to a Distinguished level to create an executive signature that is unmistakably yours.
